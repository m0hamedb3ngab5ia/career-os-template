# User requirements
What users can do, in plain words. **Start here.** You own this file.
Technical detail: REQUIREMENTS.md (REQ-NNN), each linked to the user requirement it comes from. Status lives in trace.yaml; the map at the bottom is generated by `trace.py status`.

## Getting started
### USR-001 Set up my own private copy
- Users can install the app and copy in a sample profile to start from.
- Users can run a checkup that tells them exactly what is still missing or broken.
- Users' personal data never ends up in the shared public code.
- Users can pull in updates to the shared app without losing their own data.
- Why: get running in minutes, safely

### USR-002 Upload my résumé and get feedback
- Users can easily find a section in the app to upload their résumé.
- While it uploads, users see a progress screen that shows the résumé review running in the background.
- Users get clear feedback on their résumé when the review finishes.
- Users can apply a piece of feedback with one click and the AI updates the résumé for them.
- Users can leave their own comments on the feedback instead.
- Users can also edit the résumé by hand.
- Why: a strong résumé before any applications go out

### USR-003 See what hiring systems read from my résumé
- Users can see what an applicant tracking system (ATS) would pick up from their uploaded résumé.
- Users can mark a résumé as their master résumé, which all other résumés are built from.
- Users can mark a résumé as another type instead and rename it.
- Why: know the résumé reads well to machines, and keep one source of truth

### USR-004 Keep a history of my résumés
- Users can see a list of every résumé they have uploaded.
- Users can see every version of a résumé, including who changed it: them or the AI.
- Users can open any earlier version.
- Users can delete versions they no longer want.
- Why: never lose good work and always know what changed

### USR-022 Teach the app my writing style
- In the same place as the résumé upload, users can upload past cover letters or other things they wrote.
- The app learns how users write from these samples.
- New cover letters are written in the users' own style, based on those samples.
- Users can see which samples the app is using and remove any of them.
- Why: cover letters that sound like the user, not like AI

### USR-023 Know when I'm ready to start applying
- Users see a simple checklist of what must be done before the first application goes out.
- Each item shows done or not done, with a link to where to fix it.
- Users can't start applying until the must-have items are done.
- Why: no half-ready applications going out

## Finding jobs
### USR-005 Find jobs that fit me automatically
- The app pulls new postings from the job boards users pick.
- Users can filter out jobs by title, seniority, country or company.
- Each job gets a fit score, a category and a priority tier.
- Users can see when a posting closes.
- Why: spend time only on jobs worth applying to

### USR-006 Stay safe from scams and fake jobs
- Each job gets a safe / check / blocked verdict with the reason.
- The app never fills in very sensitive details like a social security number or bank info.
- The app spots old or fake (ghost) postings.
- Users can flag or clear a company, and flagged companies stop showing up.
- Why: protect personal data and avoid wasted effort

### USR-024 Pick which jobs go into my pipeline
- Users see found jobs in a clear list with a checkbox next to each one.
- Users tick the jobs they want in their pipeline and leave the rest out.
- Users can tick or untick many jobs at once.
- Unticked jobs are never prepared or applied to.
- Users can change their mind later and add or remove a job.
- Why: users decide where their applications go

## Preparing applications
### USR-007 Get a tailored résumé and cover letter for each job
- For each good job, the app writes a one-page résumé using only the user's own experience.
- The app writes a cover letter in the user's own voice when the job calls for one.
- The app drafts answers to the application questions.
- If the result is not good enough, the app tries once more, then asks the user.
- Why: apply to more jobs without writing each one by hand

### USR-008 Trust that nothing is made up
- Every claim in a résumé, letter or answer comes from the user's own profile.
- The app checks every document for invented facts, wrong company names and private employer terms before it is used.
- The app checks that the résumé PDF matches what was reviewed.
- Why: never be embarrassed by an application

### USR-009 Don't over-apply to one company
- The app limits how many roles users apply to at one company and waits after a rejection.
- When a company has several similar roles, the best fit goes first.
- Why: look focused, not spammy, to each employer

## Applying
### USR-010 Have forms filled for me, then submit myself
- The app fills in the application form and uploads the documents.
- Users review the filled form and press Submit themselves.
- The app keeps a copy of exactly what was sent.
- Users can store job-site logins safely for the app to use.
- This works on more and more job sites over time.
- Why: save typing while keeping control

### USR-011 Optionally let the app submit for me
- Users can turn on automatic submitting as an extra option; it is off by default.
- Top-priority (dream) jobs are never submitted automatically.
- The app never submits the same application twice.
- Users set a daily limit on how many applications go out.
- Why: apply at volume when users choose to

## Running on its own
### USR-012 Let the app work overnight within my limits
- The app finds, scores and prepares jobs on a schedule, outside the hours users pick.
- Users choose how much work each run does.
- Users can pause, resume or cancel runs.
- Missed runs wait for the user instead of running by surprise.
- A job that keeps failing becomes a to-do for the user.
- Users can pick a set of jobs and choose how far the app takes them.
- Why: wake up to applications ready to review

## Staying on top of my search
### USR-013 See every job and where it stands
- Users see all jobs and their stage, from found to offer.
- Users see what needs them today on the home screen.
- Users can change a job's status, withdraw, or open its files.
- The app keeps a spreadsheet of everything up to date.
- Why: one place instead of spreadsheets and folders

### USR-014 Get a to-do list of things that need me
- Anything the app is unsure about becomes a to-do item.
- Users can mark items done, reopen them or set due dates.
- Why: the app never guesses; it asks

### USR-015 Answer a question once, never again
- When users answer an application question, the app remembers it for next time.
- The app remembers tricky steps on each job site.
- Why: less repeated work with every application

### USR-016 Reach out to people at the company
- The app finds likely recruiters and hiring managers.
- The app drafts messages; users always send them themselves.
- LinkedIn is never automated.
- Why: more interviews through real contacts

### USR-017 Have company replies tracked automatically
- The app reads job emails and updates each job's status.
- Users get a heads-up on interviews, assessments and offers.
- Why: never miss a reply

### USR-018 Change settings without editing files
- Users can change every setting in the app.
- Users can watch runs live and see their history.
- Why: no need to touch config files

### USR-019 Keep the app tidy
- The app cleans up old files on a schedule.
- The app suggests better settings; nothing changes until the user says so.
- Why: the app stays small and fast

### USR-020 See how my search is going
- Users get a weekly report: applications, replies and interview rate by job type.
- Why: know what is working

### USR-021 Use the app from my phone (later)
- Users can check to-dos, approve drafts and get alerts from their phone.
- Why: act on the go
